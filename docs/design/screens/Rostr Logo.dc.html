Turn 1 · logo directions

Five directions for the rostr mark.

All type-led, because a drawn mascot is not something I can produce well. Each is shown as a display lockup, at the 19px the site header actually uses, and as a 56px app icon — the Seeker dApp Store will need that last one regardless of which direction wins. Reference an option by its id in chat.

1a Plain wordmark Already in use
rostr
rostr r

The honest baseline. Costs nothing, survives every size, and is what the header already renders. The app icon is weak — a lone r is not memorable at 56px on a crowded home screen.

1b The o is the ball My pick
r str
r str

The ball is geometry, not illustration — one ellipse and three laces — so it stays crisp at any size and gives you a mark that works alone. It also says football instantly, which the wordmark never will. The laces drop below about 20px; keep the plain ellipse there.

1c The throw, as the mark Ties to the hero
rostr
rostr

The arc is already the site's signature gesture — the hero animation resolves into exactly this curve. Making it the logo means the mark and the motion are the same idea. Risk: an arc under a word is close to a generic swoosh, and it reads as decoration until you have seen the animation.

1d Mirrored r’s Riskiest
rost r
rost r r r

Flipping the last r turns the name into a symmetrical pair and gives you a real two-letter monogram for the icon. It also makes the word harder to read at a glance, and people will ask whether it is a typo. Include it because the icon is genuinely the best of the five; do not ship it without testing that.

1e Ball as the counter Icon-first
rostr
rostr

A separate mark beside the word — the most conventional structure, and the most flexible: the tile works alone as a favicon, an avatar and a dApp Store icon without any of the compromises the other four make. Least distinctive of the five, and the one most likely to look like other sports apps.

What I’d do

Take 1b. It is the only one where the mark and the word are a single object, it says football without a picture of anything, and the ellipse survives down to a favicon. Pair it with 1e’s tile for places that need a square.

On dark and light

Everything here is drawn on Nocturne’s dark ground. All five need a light-background version before use — the accent at #9184d9 holds up on white, but the accent-tinted tiles do not. Say the word and I’ll do a light sheet.

Caveat

These are drawn with type and simple shapes, which is what I can do well. If you want something illustrated — a helmet, a figure, a crest — that needs a real designer or an image tool, not me.
