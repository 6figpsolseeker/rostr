Mobile web · 390px

Thirty-four screens at the narrowest common phone width, in a mobile browser. Not a reflow of the desktop layouts — the desktop two-column shell has no mobile equivalent, so navigation moves to a bottom bar within thumb reach, tables become stacked rows, and each screen commits to one primary action pinned above the bar. Every tap target is at least 44px.

What changed from desktop, and why

Navigation moves to the bottom

The desktop tab row sits under a header nobody can reach one-handed. Five destinations at 56px each, the sixth and beyond behind League.

The draft board becomes a tab

Twelve columns will not fit at any legible size, and shrinking it produces something nobody can read under a 90-second clock. On a phone the clock, the need line and one-tap draft are the screen; the board is a place you go to look.

No drag-and-drop anywhere

Dragging a row inside a scrolling page is unreliable on touch. Tap a slot, choose from a sheet — the same two taps, and it cannot be started by accident while scrolling.

Tables become rows

Three columns maximum, the third right-aligned and numeric. Everything else — team, status, ADP — collapses into a second line under the name.

One primary action, pinned

Save lineup, Draft, Sign and join. Full-width at 48px above the bar, so it never scrolls out of reach mid-decision.

Long team names truncate

Every name cell is minmax(0, 1fr) with ellipsis. "Tuesday Night Regrets" is 21 characters and the field allows more — a wrapping name must never push a score off the row.

Every shipped route now has a mobile design, including the ones desktop still lacks: the Leagues hub, player detail, full standings, matchup, sign in and account setup.
